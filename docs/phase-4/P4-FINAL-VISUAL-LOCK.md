# P4 — Final Visual Lock

**Status: LOCKED — P4-FD1 planning deliverable, 30 September 2026.** Final direction: **Premium Civic-Academic Interface**. Source implementation, font installation, and FD2 have not started. This document is a specification, not a claim that the new system has been rendered or accepted.

## 1. Authority, scope, and design thesis

The latest FD1 brief authorizes this visual amendment. Read `DFCAMCLP.md`, Phase 4 context, the old design system, UX rules, roadmap, the [FD1 audit](P4-FD1-FINAL-VISUAL-AUDIT.md), then this lock before implementation. This file supersedes only the visual rules explicitly listed in §12. `DFCAMCLP.md` still owns institution facts; `P4-UX-RULES.md` still owns behavior. Historical completion reports remain historical.

Public pages should feel like a well-edited college prospectus with a real place behind it: campus photograph, original seal, confident editorial headings, compact program registers, and clear admissions information. Authenticated pages should feel like carefully organized academic work: a definite shell, visible section boundaries, strong entity context, and space allocated to the task. The two share typography, palette, rules, controls, and institutional restraint.

Richness comes from proportion and contrast. The recognizable motif is a **blue institutional anchor with a short yellow keyline**, supported by cool neutral surfaces and dark text. It is not a colored-card theme. Keep the campus hero as the primary expressive image; add no new image, icon library, background texture, logo variant, or decorative graphic.

Routes, query contracts, workflows, fields, validation rules, source data, fixture values, auth/access, permissions, search/filter/sort behavior, and canonical school facts remain unchanged. Recomposition may regroup existing content visually; it may not remove controls, introduce hidden filter drawers, change default selection, or add new workflow steps. Preserve all sample/unofficial/V1 ASSUMPTION meanings. No new packages, services, backend work, dark theme, or deployment are implied.

## 2. Typography — one direction selected

### Comparison and decision

| Candidate                                | Fit for this product                                                                                                  | Tradeoff                                                                                           | Decision                            |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Source Sans 3 + selective Source Serif 4 | Interface text stays practical; public headings gain a distinct academic/editorial voice within a related family pair | Two font assets/families require controlled scope and loading verification                         | **Selected**                        |
| Source Sans 3 alone                      | Coherent and economical; improves interface rhythm without a second face                                              | Public differentiation would depend entirely on scale/composition and remain closer to the portals | Rejected as the final art direction |
| IBM Plex Sans + selective IBM Plex Serif | Strong character and useful family relationship; credible for technical operations                                    | Its overt technical/corporate character is a weaker fit for the civic, student-facing public pages | Rejected as the final art direction |

These are design judgments, not measured usability findings. Adobe describes Source Sans 3 as designed for UI environments and Source Serif as its companion. IBM describes Plex as its corporate typeface. Primary references: [Source Sans](https://github.com/adobe-fonts/source-sans), [Source Serif](https://github.com/adobe-fonts/source-serif), and [IBM Plex](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/typography/typeface/). No candidate font was installed or rendered in the app in FD1; typography fit must pass the FD2 golden review without reopening a font tournament.

**Lock:** Source Sans 3 for all interface text, authenticated headings, tables, controls, auth forms, metadata, technical references, and document previews. Source Serif 4 only for public hero/interior titles and major editorial section headings. No serif labels, tables, portal greetings, counts, form titles, or UI badges. No italic emphasis added as decoration. No third designed family or monospace theme for Technology.

Future FD2 delivery uses self-hosted WOFF2 font assets from the official repositories with their license notices; no runtime third-party font request and no font package. Use upright Source Sans weights 400/600/700 and Source Serif 600. A variable asset may supply those values without authorizing arbitrary weights. Preserve accented names and ñ/Ñ. Load the serif only on public editorial surfaces; use sensible system sans and Georgia/serif fallbacks with a stable layout. Verify the actual selected font, late-load behavior, fallback wrapping, and absence of synthetic bold. If asset access is blocked, report that implementation blocker; do not silently ship Arial as the chosen design.

Use normal body tracking, at most −0.015em on large headings, sentence case, and no routine uppercase eyebrow. Preserve literal canonical acronyms and document warnings. Keep body paragraphs at 60–72 characters, never force full-width prose. Use tabular numerals for aligned counts, grades, dates, and time columns. IDs, status words, task actions, and essential disclaimers must not fall to 12px. Long names wrap at word boundaries; reserve atomic code/time width instead of breaking BSCpE or separating AM/PM from its time. No ellipsis as the only access to essential information.

## 3. Color and surfaces

Canonical blue **#0D13CD** and yellow **#FCDF00** remain unchanged. Green remains semantic success only. A few stronger neutral roles provide depth without covering the interface in brand color.

| Role                     | Value     | Locked usage                                                                                      |
| ------------------------ | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Canvas                   | `#EDF0F4` | Authenticated page background and quiet public transitions                                        |
| Base surface             | `#FFFFFF` | Work region, form, table body, editorial reading section                                          |
| Raised surface           | `#FFFFFF` | Menus/dialogs; distinguished by border and elevation, not an unrelated tint                       |
| Inset surface            | `#E3E8EF` | Toolbars, table headers, technical reference bands, supporting metadata groups                    |
| Restrained brand surface | `#EEF0FF` | Selected item/current task emphasis; never every card                                             |
| Main ink                 | `#17233B` | Headings, body, key data; also compact masthead/portal identity text                              |
| Secondary ink            | `#4A5870` | Metadata, help, descriptions on light surfaces                                                    |
| Brand                    | `#0D13CD` | Primary action, active edge, short identity anchor, focus and navigation emphasis                 |
| Brand hover              | `#0A0FA4` | Primary hover/pressed reinforcement; no fading enabled controls                                   |
| Accent                   | `#FCDF00` | Short keyline against blue/dark identity anchor; occasional labeled emphasis with dark text       |
| Accent soft              | `#FFF9CC` | Existing sample-document disclosure or deliberate neutral context emphasis; not universal warning |

**Blue:** give it a stable institutional home in the masthead/portal identity block, primary actions, and selected navigation. Use dark ink for static dates, counts, subject names, and route text. Ordinary prose/row links retain blue and a persistent underline; visual restraint comes from removing color from nonlinks, not removing link affordance. Existing button/link semantics remain.

**Yellow:** replace decorative notice/heading dots with a 2px keyline of 32–48px against the blue institutional anchor. Use one anchor per shell; do not repeat yellow on every section. Yellow is not a small-text color on white, a warning color, a progress state, or a universal focus indicator. Existing meaningful calendar-event dots can stay; the rule targets decorative brand dots. Existing sample-document strips remain clearly labeled and need not become yellow on every page.

**Semantic colors:** retain the existing success/warning/danger/information/neutral meanings and darker text/soft-fill pairs from the old system. Do not invent a new status palette or recolor “Not Qualified” as a technical failure. Closed remains neutral; priority remains distinct from ticket status. Tight badge geometry may change, meaning may not.

Calculated flat-color checks: white on brand blue 10.54:1; main ink on white 15.67:1; secondary ink on canvas 6.29:1; main ink on yellow 11.71:1; yellow against blue 7.88:1. These arithmetic checks support the proposed palette, not a rendered accessibility pass. Base/canvas contrast is only 1.14:1 (old pair approximately 1.05:1), so surfaces still need structural borders where separation matters. No fill contrast alone may communicate an interactive state.

### Surface decision rule

Use a plain section when its heading and rule already group the content. Use a base surface for a distinct task or data region. Use inset fill for subordinate controls/facts within that region without a second card. Use brand tint only for a current/selected region. Use raised surfaces for overlays, not ordinary information. Independent form and activity regions may remain separate; flattening must not imply they are the same task.

Reserve 2px neutral rules for structure; inputs stay 1px except focus/invalid treatment. Do not draw a box around each paragraph.

Geometry: **6px controls, 8px independent panels and overlays, 4px badges, 0px plain rules/table internal corners**. Avatars remain circular. No capsules for navigation, page titles, cards, or primary actions. Geometry follows component purpose within one family; do not create portal-specific radii.

## 6. Shell — institutional frame, operational interior

**Top bar:** continuous opaque white masthead, 64px desktop height and at least 64px mobile, allowed to grow for long labels/zoom. Replace the public floating rounded header container with the same aligned masthead language. Keep one supplied seal at 40px, original colors/aspect ratio, beside DFCAMCLP. Use main ink for the wordmark, canonical blue for a short institutional anchor, and the bounded yellow keyline. The rest of the header remains light; no full-screen or full-sidebar brand fill.

**Sidebar:** keep 256px desktop width and existing breakpoint/navigation model. Use a visibly separate light base with a strong right divider. The upper identity block names the portal at 20px/600, role at 14px when different, then a compact blue identity anchor/keyline. Preserve Applicant demo tools in their existing navigation slot. Navigation is 15px, at least 44px high, with 12–16px horizontal padding. Current item uses restrained blue fill, 3px edge, heavier text, and its existing programmatic current state. The role is not a status badge or permission editor. Do not repeat Applicant/Applicant or Student/Student.

**Account area:** keep the existing Account menu and conditional authorized portal switcher. Give the Account trigger a quiet boundary/hover state and label it **Account** on mobile as well as desktop; the separate hamburger opens navigation. Use existing identity information in the menu, a structural separator, and an explicit Sign out action. Do not add profile fields or an account workflow. The visible naming correction clarifies the existing affordance without changing behavior.

**Mobile:** preserve the current native dialog drawer, close action, focus behavior, authorized links and portal choices. Give it the same identity block, active edge and account separation. Keep the Applicant selector accessible and truthful. Public navigation remains its existing nonmodal disclosure. No bottom-navigation replacement, hover-only destination, or inferred access.

Logo destinations remain governed by UX rules. Neutral account/denial/404 surfaces use public identity and safe existing return links; never guess an authorized portal from a failed URL. Retain sticky behavior only where it already exists; no additional sticky chrome as a side effect of this pass.

## 10. Responsive and interaction rules

The golden baseline pairs **1920×1080 and 375×812**. Implementation also checks **1440×900 and 768×900**, retaining the old acceptance sizes, plus 320 CSS px reflow and text zoom where appropriate. Responsive Chromium evidence is not physical-device or Safari evidence.

At 375px, one main task column does not mean one card per section. Use shared base regions with ruled rows, compact facts and readable type. Reduce accumulated introductory space, not touch targets or font sizes. At default text size, target first meaningful dashboard task/action within the initial 812px viewport and the first directory result by approximately one viewport where existing filter count permits. For dense forms/filters, record justified overflow rather than hiding controls to hit that target. Larger text takes precedence over fold targets.

At tablet widths, wrap controls and collapse master/detail when column minima fail. Retain the existing shell breakpoint/drawer behavior. At large desktop, use the width roles in §5. Restrict horizontal scrolling to existing tab/day rails and genuine comparison tables; never page-level overflow. Native selects may have OS-dependent menus; the surrounding control still follows the visual recipe.

Focus remains distinguishable from hover/selection. Preserve skip link, labels, semantic heading order, table associations, dialog behavior, tab keys, live feedback, and all existing actions. FD2–FD4 add no new scroll/entrance motion. Existing 150ms color/border transitions remain; M7 alone owns final motion. Reduced motion must show complete content immediately.

## 11. Anti-patterns

Do not use glassmorphism, neon, decorative gradients, giant shadows, huge radii, colorful dashboard-card grids, ornamental icons, tiny yellow dots, or charts without existing task/data purpose. Do not replace blue with a fashionable alternative, tint every portal differently, or add a third font. Avoid broad cream/serif luxury styling: serif has a bounded institutional role; operations remain precise Sans interfaces.

Do not hide sample limitations, remove meaningful labels to shorten a page, shrink staff tables below readable sizes, hide overflowing names, create new workflows, or use a style pass to correct fixtures. Do not treat the screenshot filenames as canonical subject names: state 0182's legacy URL says IS 203 while its rendered class is IS 304; retain the actual data/query contract. No decorative animation to make a weak composition feel finished.

## 14. Visual acceptance and handoff

FD1 passes when the audit is evidence-backed, one direction is selected, the old-rule amendments are explicit, all 35 golden entries resolve, and only the requested documentation changes. This does not grant FD2 execution.

Future visual acceptance requires:

1. **Identity continuity:** public hero and lower sections share the locked heading/rule/palette language; authenticated masthead/sidebar feel related. One unaltered seal per shell, no duplicate auth seal, purposeful yellow keyline, no decorative yellow dots.
2. **Type:** loaded Source Sans/Serif match §2 roles; headings wrap naturally at 375 and 1920; no synthetic style, missing glyph, layout-clipping fallback, or essential 12px text. Public BSCpE and calendar time strings remain intact.
3. **Surfaces/structure:** canvas, base, inset and raised roles are visibly different at normal viewing scale. Major section rules and table headers remain visible without zoom. No repeated card-wrapper stack around one task.
4. **Density:** Technology account identity gains adequate width at 1920; headers/toolbars/results align. Mobile dashboard preambles no longer consume most of the first screen. Forms/reading stay constrained. No filler content introduced to occupy space.
5. **Shell:** portal and role can be located immediately; active navigation is unmistakable without color alone; Account is distinct from navigation. Existing authorized choices, logo destinations and drawer behavior are preserved.
6. **Components/states:** consistent controls, visible labels, flat active-tab language, ruled mobile lists, consistent dialogs. Empty, invalid credentials, validation, denied and 404 all have deliberate presentation and existing/safe recovery. Long document dialog controls remain reachable.
