## 2. Diagnosis

The product now has a credible academic identity: campus photography, selective editorial serif, strong table structure, and genuinely different portal tasks. It still feels inert because expressive feedback is concentrated in a few overlays, yellow almost disappears outside the seal/keyline, and several Applicant layouts reserve wide containers while using only a narrow part of them. This is a refinement of a sound system, not grounds for a new dashboard template.

### Keep what already works

Keep the original campus image and seal, Source Sans 3/Source Serif 4 roles, cool light surfaces, 6/8/4px geometry, readable form fields, explicit sample documents, ordered admissions process, calm Not Qualified state, Student timetable/agenda, staff queue/table widths, and Technology's inline mobile selected detail. A short 404, denied state, empty agenda or two-record list may legitimately leave whitespace. Do not fill those screens or animate empty space.

The cognitive burden is not an excessive number of top-level features: Applicant's six destinations have distinct purposes. The burden comes from repeated introductory layers and weak location of secondary controls. Records' several independent filters are useful choices, not automatically an error for exceeding four options; keep them visible and labeled. An applicant's emotional peak should be understanding the next step, and the ending should be a truthful confirmation. Do not turn admission outcomes or grade saves into celebrations.

First-time Applicant risk: an account photo could be mistaken for a required admissions upload; label its local presentation purpose. Keyboard user risk: animated tabs/menus could move or hide focus; immediate focus and selection are mandatory. Returning staff risk: repeated page entrances could make ordinary work feel slower; exclude rows, rosters and form entry from reveal sequences.
